BDC Mark Tape

BDCs (stock-listed funds that lend to private companies) publish every loan they hold. This tool puts those public reports into one table. Lenders use it to price a new loan and to pick which existing loans to check first. Every number links to its SEC filing.

Project codename: Parallax.

Problem

Private lenders can't see what other lenders charge or how they value similar loans. So a proposed interest margin goes to the approval committee on hearsay. And a borrower other lenders already value low waits for review.

Example: public reports hold 90 first-lien insurance loans from 4 lenders, at a median of S+475. Finding them by hand means reading 64 filings.

For

OrganisationA lender to mid-sized private companies, such as a private credit fund (the product spec's worked example is the person pricing loans at such a lender)
UserThe person pricing a new loan: which margin to defend at the approval committee for a borrower in a given industry
UserThe person reviewing existing loans: which 15 borrowers to check this quarter
BuyerHead of lending (assumed, not in the source docs): whether to pay for the table and the reviewer time to run it

Terms on this page

  • BDC: a stock-listed fund that lends to private companies.
  • Valuation (the "mark"): the lender's own estimate of a loan's worth, as a share of what it paid.
  • Interest margin (spread): what a lender charges above a benchmark rate. S+475 means the benchmark plus 475 hundredths of a percent.
  • First lien: a loan that is repaid first if the borrower fails.
  • Non-accrual: the lender stops counting a loan's interest as income, because payment is in doubt.
  • Name matching: linking one borrower that each lender spells differently.
  • Hit rate (precision@15): of 15 flagged borrowers, the share that went non-accrual the next quarter.
  • AUC: how well a score ranks future problem loans ahead of the rest. Higher is better.

User stories

  1. As the person pricing a new loan, I want the margins and valuations other BDCs report for the same industry, with the lenders and source rows, so that I can back my margin at committee and see when the "market" is one lender's loans.
  2. As the person reviewing existing loans, I want a quarterly list sized to my time, lowest valuation first, with what changed and a link to each filing, so that my time goes to the borrowers other lenders already value low.

Try it

  1. Run it locally: pip install -r requirements.txt, then streamlit run app/two_stories.py from the showcase folder.
  2. Price a loan: pick Software, first_lien. Check the number of loans and lenders before the median. Switch to Software and Services to see the warning that every loan comes from one lender.
  3. Review list: pick 2025-12-31 and 15 names. Open one borrower to see each lender's valuation and filing. Then read the hindsight check below the list: which flagged borrowers went non-accrual.

Data: real public SEC filings, a fixed copy taken 2026-09-06. No AI language model, no model score, no login. Nothing goes online except SEC links you choose to open. Costs $0 to run.

Value equation

value = (outcome x likelihood) / (time delay x effort & sacrifice)

PERSON PRICING A NEW LOAN

Outcome
"My margin sits at the median of 90 first-lien insurance loans across 4 BDCs. Here are the rows."
Likelihood
MEDIUM. Rows and filings can be checked. Many industries have few loans: Software first lien has 5 loans from 2 lenders. Not tested with real users.
Delay
LOW. The answer shows on selection, from a saved file. Latest quarter in the copy is 2026-06-30. The copy is dated 2026-09-06.
Effort
MEDIUM. The user guesses which industry name in the filings fits the borrower. Industry names are never merged.
Sacrifice
LOW. Public data only. No full yield: fees and upfront discounts (OID) aren't in the filings.

PERSON REVIEWING LOANS

Outcome
"My 15 borrowers this quarter are the ones lenders value lowest, with what moved and the filing one click away."
Likelihood
LOW-MEDIUM. Looking back, 3 of 15 (2025-12-31) and 1 of 15 (2026-03-31) went non-accrual the next quarter. Wide uncertainty ranges.
Delay
HIGH. The next filing shows whether the list was right: one quarter later.
Effort
LOW. One sorted list, sized to the time available.
Sacrifice
MEDIUM. Covers only borrowers of 8 BDCs, not the reviewer's own loans. No machine-learning score.

HEAD OF LENDING (BUYER)

Outcome
"Margins at committee cite what other lenders charge, and review time goes to the lowest valuations."
Likelihood
LOW. No user tests. Release checks failed (D-018).
Delay
HIGH. Effects on pricing and money recovered show over several quarters.
Effort
MEDIUM. Someone matches the table to the fund's own loans and keeps the filing reader working each quarter.
Sacrifice
LOW. Public data, no data vendor to depend on, $0 to run.

Verdict: the weakest factor for all three is likelihood. Cheapest fix for the pricer: show screen 1 to two pricers and count who'd bring it to committee. The product spec already names this test. Cheapest fix for the reviewer: show the hindsight check for every quarter beside the list. The demo now does this, so belief rests on results for any of 7 quarters, not a claim. For the buyer: fix the failed release checks before any adoption talk.

Sub-problems
  1. No single table. Valuations and margins sit in 64 web-page filings with 8 different table layouts.
  2. Names don't match. Each lender spells the same borrower differently, so their valuations can't be compared.
  3. Comparisons have no sources. A margin without its lenders and rows can't be defended at committee. (Story 1)
  4. One lender looks like a market. Each BDC names industries its own way, so most industry groups hold one lender's loans. (Story 1)
  5. Review list isn't sized or ordered. A reviewer with time for 15 borrowers has no ranked list. (Story 2)
  6. What changed is hidden. Valuation moves, lender disagreement and new names need a quarter-on-quarter comparison and the filing. (Story 2)
  7. Warning before the valuation falls. Predicting non-accrual earlier than the lowest valuation does.
  8. Full pricing. Fees, upfront discounts (OID) and early-repayment terms aren't in the loan lists.
  9. Coverage. Only 8 BDCs and 8 quarters. No banks, private funds or names of the buyout firms that own borrowers.
  10. Investor valuation check. Whether a fund manager's valuation agrees with other lenders to the same borrower.
  11. Industry mapping. One shared list of industry names across lenders.

How it works
  1. Download the annual (10-K) and quarterly (10-Q) reports of ARCC, OBDC, FSK, GBDC, MAIN, TSLX, HTGC and PSEC from SEC EDGAR. Save and index them: 64 filings.
  2. Find each filing's loan list (Schedule of Investments) and read it. Result: 42,830 loan rows with margin, amount owed, cost, fair value, non-accrual flag and SEC filing ID.
  3. Match borrower names: clean them, group likely pairs, score how alike they are, and link names scoring 93 or more. Result: 2,348 borrowers.
  4. Build one row per borrower per quarter: lowest lender valuation (fvc_min), change since last quarter, lender disagreement, and whether it went non-accrual next quarter.
  5. Story 1: pick industry, loan type and size. The tool takes exact industry-name matches from each lender's latest quarter. It shows quartiles, a lender table and source rows with SEC links.
  6. Story 2: pick a quarter and how many borrowers you can review. The tool sorts by fvc_min and shows change, a NEW marker, each lender's valuation, filing links and the hindsight check.

Valuation = total fair value / total cost, per lender, on loan rows only. Netting rows, equity and rows with zero cost are left out.

Hypotheses, one per tackled sub-problem

1 · NO SINGLE TABLE

Before -> after
64 filings read one by one -> one table of 42,830 loans, each row with its SEC filing ID
Hypothesis
If every loan list is read into one format, then comparisons and review lists work across 8 lenders, because the filings already hold each loan's valuation and margin.
Metric
Accuracy of the money columns on a fixed check sample. Up. Starting point: 48 of 48 correct on amount owed, cost and fair value. Floor: below 0.90 on any money column means drop the table (README rule).
Test
Draw a new sample each quarter and check rows against the filing text.

2 · NAMES DON'T MATCH

Before -> after
each lender's spelling -> one borrower ID across lenders
Hypothesis
If names are matched with measured accuracy, then one borrower's valuations from several lenders sit side by side, because a wrong match would show a fake disagreement.
Metric
Share of matched pairs that are correct (precision), at the chosen threshold. Up. Starting point 0.9855, n=69. Floor: precision of whole matched groups, starting point 0.85, n=20.
Test
New hand-labelled sample, threshold fixed before labelling. Below 0.95 means stop (README rule).

3 · COMPARISONS WITHOUT SOURCES

Before -> after
a quoted margin -> quartiles, lender table and source rows with SEC links
Hypothesis
If the margin comes with its lenders and rows, then the pricer answers the committee's "says who?" from the screen, because each row links to the filing.
Metric
Time to answer where a proposed margin comes from. Down. Starting point unobserved.
Test
Two pricers, 5 practice committee questions, broker notes vs the screen.

4 · ONE LENDER AS A MARKET

Before -> after
a group median read as the market -> lender count first, one-lender warning, similar industry names listed but not merged
Hypothesis
If the screen names which lender's loans drive the number, then fewer one-lender medians reach committee as "market", because the warning appears before the quartiles.
Metric
Share of cited comparisons resting on one lender. Down. Starting point unobserved.
Test
Same practice session: count medians cited without their lender count.

5 · UNSIZED REVIEW LIST

Before -> after
no ranked list -> list sized to review time, lowest lender valuation first
Hypothesis
If the reviewer checks the 15 lowest-valued borrowers, then more of them go non-accrual next quarter than a random 15, because a lender valuing a loan below cost is publicly admitting trouble.
Metric
Share of the 15 flagged that went non-accrual next quarter (precision@15). Up. Starting point: 3 of 15 (2025-12-31), 1 of 15 (2026-03-31), against overall rates of 22 of 1,423 and 19 of 1,415. The reviewer's own list: unobserved.
Test
Reviewer's list vs this list on the same quarter, result from the next filing.

6 · HIDDEN CHANGE

Before -> after
reopen last quarter's filing -> valuation change, NEW marker, each lender's valuation and filing link per row
Hypothesis
If each row shows what moved and links its filing, then the reviewer spends less time per borrower before escalating, because the change and source sit on one line.
Metric
Minutes per reviewed borrower. Down. Starting point unobserved.
Test
Time one reviewer on 15 borrowers with filings only vs with the list.

Terms. Non-accrual: the lender stops counting the loan's interest as income. NEW: not on the prior quarter's list of the same length. It doesn't mean newly in trouble.

Observed in the build
  • Coverage: 8 BDCs, 8 quarter ends from 2024-09-30 to 2026-06-30, 64 filings (16 10-K, 48 10-Q), 42,830 loan rows, 2,348 matched borrowers. Source: tools.coverage() on showcase/data. Supports 1.
  • Reading check: 48 of 48 correct on amount owed, cost and fair value, Wilson 95% confidence interval [0.9259, 1.0000] each. The person who wrote the reader drew the sample, from the same data. Source: eval/parse_audit_results.json. Supports 1.
  • Name matching: pair precision 0.9855 (68 of 69), CI [0.9224, 0.9974]; recall (share of true matches found) 0.9315 on 73 labelled true pairs; group precision 0.85 (17 of 20), CI [0.6396, 0.9476]. Threshold 93 was chosen on the same sample. 187 borrowers held by 2+ BDCs at 2026-06-30. Source: eval/er_results.json. Supports 2.
  • Story 1, Software first lien: 5 loans, 2 lenders (GBDC 4, PSEC 1), median S+675. Too few to rely on. Source: get_comparables, asserted in tests/test_showcase.py. Supports 3, 4.
  • Story 1, Insurance first lien: 90 loans, 4 lenders, median S+475; ARCC supplies 52 of 90. Source: two_stories.lender_table. Supports 3, 4.
  • Story 1, Software and Services first lien: 193 loans, all from ARCC: one lender's loans. Source: get_comparables. Supports 4.
  • Story 2, 2026-03-31: 15 borrowers, 5 new since 2025-12-31; 1 of 15 went non-accrual next quarter, Wilson CI [0.01, 0.30]; overall rate 19 of 1,415. Source: two_stories.hindsight. Supports 5, 6.
  • Story 2, 2025-12-31: 3 of 15, CI [0.07, 0.45]; overall rate 22 of 1,423. Both quarters match the saved simple-rule precision@15 (0.200, 0.067) in eval/model_results.json, checked by a test. Supports 5.
  • Story 2, all 7 quarters: listed borrowers with a known outcome that went non-accrual range from 0 to 4. Three quarters show 0. Weak support for 5.
  • Tests: 12 passed (7 new for the two-story app, 5 existing), including checks that the app loads with no error (AppTest). The local server health check returned 200.
Limits and trade-offs
  • Machine-learning result: an XGBoost model did not beat the simple rule of ranking by lowest valuation. Tested on 2,838 borrower-periods kept out of training, 41 of them non-accrual. AUC 0.8160 simple rule vs 0.7288 model; difference -0.0860, CI [-0.1948, 0.0273]. No difference range excludes zero, so no gap is proven. Source: eval/model_results.json.
  • Failed release checks: saved model scores don't match the model they cite, on all 2,838 rows. Checks that trace past data to its source (lineage hashes, an ancestor manifest) also fail (M6.4). Cycle 11 ended LOOP, escalated to STOP (D-018). The demo hides the scores. That hides the defect; it doesn't fix it.
  • Criteria state: 35 criteria in team/criteria.json v9: 27 PASS, 3 FAIL, 5 PENDING. The three product criteria (P1 to P3) are PENDING.
  • Users: no pricer or reviewer has used it. Every "before" is assumed.
  • Small samples: 15 borrowers per quarter; one borrower moves precision by 0.067.
  • Data age: latest quarter in the table is 2026-06-30. The review data stops at 2026-03-31, because each outcome needs the next filing.
  • Reader limits: no currency column; some industry names that look like dates are hidden from the picker; one OBDC filing's totals differ by -1.17%. Source: LIMITATIONS.md.
  • Funds as borrowers: fund and joint-venture vehicles can appear on the review list; they aren't removed.
  • Visual review: no person has checked how the screens look; tests check page text.
