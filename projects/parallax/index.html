Case study · Private credit · Public SEC data

What other lenders charge and how they value the same loans, with the filing behind every number.

You price or review loans at a lender to mid-sized private companies. You can't see what other lenders charge, so your margin goes to the approval committee on hearsay. BDC Mark Tape puts the loans that BDCs (stock-listed funds that lend to private companies) publish into one table you can check.

01 · VISION

Every margin at committee cites what other lenders charge.

BDCs already publish every loan they hold, with its margin and valuation. It sits in 64 filings with 8 table layouts. The vision: one table of those loans, matched by borrower, so your margin and your review list rest on rows anyone can check.

02 · THE PROBLEM

Six things go wrong today.

1 · No single table

Valuations and margins sit in 64 filings with 8 table layouts, so you can't compare lenders.

2 · Names don't match

Each lender spells the same borrower differently, so their valuations can't sit side by side.

3 · Your margin has no source

A margin without its lenders and rows can't be defended at committee.

4 · One lender looks like a market

Each BDC names industries its own way, so most industry groups hold one lender's loans.

5 · Your review time goes unranked

With time for 15 borrowers, you have no list sorted by how other lenders value them.

6 · What changed is hidden

Valuation moves and new names need last quarter's filing open next to this one.

03 · THE SOLUTION

Code reads the filings. You price and review from the rows.

  1. Every loan list is read into one table. 64 annual and quarterly reports of 8 BDCs from SEC EDGAR: margin, amount owed, cost, fair value, non-accrual flag and SEC filing ID per row.
  2. Borrower names are matched across lenders. Names scoring 93 or more are linked: 2,348 borrowers.
  3. Price a loan. Pick industry, loan type and size. You see the lender count first, then quartiles, a lender table and source rows with SEC links. A warning shows when every loan comes from one lender.
  4. Review the right 15. Pick a quarter and how many borrowers you can review. You get the lowest valuations first, what changed, each lender's valuation, and a hindsight check of which flagged borrowers went non-accrual.

05 · THE VALUE

What it's worth, and what it asks of you.

Public reports hold 90 first-lien insurance loans from 4 lenders, at a median of S+475. Finding them by hand means reading 64 filings.

You, pricing a new loan
You get
"My margin sits at the median of 90 first-lien insurance loans across 4 BDCs. Here are the rows."
Will you believe it?
Medium. Rows and filings can be checked. Many industries have few loans: Software first lien has 5 loans from 2 lenders.

Low delay. The answer shows on selection. Latest quarter in the copy is 2026-06-30.

Medium. You guess which industry name in the filings fits the borrower. Industry names are never merged.

Low to medium. Looking back, 3 of 15 (2025-12-31) and 1 of 15 (2026-03-31) went non-accrual the next quarter. Wide uncertainty ranges.

High delay. The next filing shows whether the list was right: one quarter later.
What it takes
Low. One sorted list, sized to your time.
What you give up
Medium. It covers only borrowers of 8 BDCs, not your own loans. No machine-learning score.

06 · LIMITATIONS

What this doesn't prove yet.

  • Machine-learning result: an XGBoost model did not beat the simple rule of ranking by lowest valuation. Tested on 2,838 borrower-periods kept out of training, 41 of them non-accrual. AUC 0.8160 simple rule vs 0.7288 model; difference -0.0860, CI [-0.1948, 0.0273]. No difference range excludes zero, so no gap is proven. Source: eval/model_results.json.
  • Failed release checks: saved model scores don't match the model they cite, on all 2,838 rows. Checks that trace past data to its source (lineage hashes, an ancestor manifest) also fail (M6.4). Cycle 11 ended LOOP, escalated to STOP (D-018). The demo hides the scores. That hides the defect; it doesn't fix it.
  • Criteria state: 35 criteria in team/criteria.json v9: 27 PASS, 3 FAIL, 5 PENDING. The three product criteria (P1 to P3) are PENDING.
  • Users: no pricer or reviewer has used it. Every "before" is assumed.
  • Small samples: 15 borrowers per quarter; one borrower moves precision by 0.067.
  • Data age: latest quarter in the table is 2026-06-30. The review data stops at 2026-03-31, because each outcome needs the next filing.
  • Reader limits: no currency column; some industry names that look like dates are hidden from the picker; one OBDC filing's totals differ by -1.17%. Source: LIMITATIONS.md.
  • Funds as borrowers: fund and joint-venture vehicles can appear on the review list; they aren't removed.
  • Visual review: no person has checked how the screens look; tests check page text.

How it's built download, read, match names, build, two stories
  1. Download the annual (10-K) and quarterly (10-Q) reports of ARCC, OBDC, FSK, GBDC, MAIN, TSLX, HTGC and PSEC from SEC EDGAR. Save and index them: 64 filings.
  2. Find each filing's loan list (Schedule of Investments) and read it. Result: 42,830 loan rows with margin, amount owed, cost, fair value, non-accrual flag and SEC filing ID.
  3. Match borrower names: clean them, group likely pairs, score how alike they are, and link names scoring 93 or more. Result: 2,348 borrowers.
  4. Build one row per borrower per quarter: lowest lender valuation (fvc_min), change since last quarter, lender disagreement, and whether it went non-accrual next quarter.
  5. Story 1: pick industry, loan type and size. The tool takes exact industry-name matches from each lender's latest quarter. It shows quartiles, a lender table and source rows with SEC links.
  6. Story 2: pick a quarter and how many borrowers you can review. The tool sorts by fvc_min and shows change, a NEW marker, each lender's valuation, filing links and the hindsight check.

Valuation = total fair value / total cost, per lender, on loan rows only. Netting rows, equity and rows with zero cost are left out.

Terms on this page plain definitions
  • BDC: a stock-listed fund that lends to private companies.
  • Valuation (the "mark"): the lender's own estimate of a loan's worth, as a share of what it paid.
  • Interest margin (spread): what a lender charges above a benchmark rate. S+475 means the benchmark plus 475 hundredths of a percent.
  • First lien: a loan that is repaid first if the borrower fails.
  • Non-accrual: the lender stops counting a loan's interest as income, because payment is in doubt.
  • Name matching: linking one borrower that each lender spells differently.
  • Hit rate (precision@15): of 15 flagged borrowers, the share that went non-accrual the next quarter.
  • AUC: how well a score ranks future problem loans ahead of the rest. Higher is better.

1) Problem and hypothesis

Problem. Private-credit loans are priced and marked behind closed doors. The only public, position-level record is the Schedule of Investments each listed BDC files quarterly, and nobody has stitched those into one tape where the same borrower is visible across lenders.

Hypothesis. If we parse the SOI from 8 BDCs over 8 quarters, resolve borrowers across filers with measured precision, and put a model and a cited agent on top, then an underwriter can defend a spread with BDC-observed comparables (what 8 lenders hold, not "the market"), a monitoring analyst can pick 15 names with a stated hit rate, and an allocator can compare a manager's mark with other holders', because a lender's fair-value-to-cost mark is its own public admission of stress, one holder often marks a borrower down before the others do, and that disagreement only exists once entities are resolved.

The allocator gets no screen: the mark-dispersion query (M4) and get_borrower (M7) serve that job, checked by P3.

Supporting evidence. The brief, nothing else. No user interviews yet; the jobs below stay hypotheses until a practitioner confirms them (open question 1).

Jobs to be done (verbatim from the brief):

Persona Job
Deal underwriter When pricing a deal, know what the market is actually clearing at for a comparable borrower, to defend a spread to the investment committee.
Monitoring analyst With 200 borrowers and capacity to review 15, pick the right 15, early enough to preserve recovery.
Allocator (free output) Is my manager's mark consistent with how other lenders mark the same borrower?

Strategic fit. The brief claims an observable private-credit tape doesn't exist in public form. Every input is a public SEC filing, so the data isn't the moat. The moat is the measured pipeline: parse audit, entity resolution with precision and recall, a model at the operating point a real analyst has (15 reviews a quarter), an agent that cites or refuses. One spine serves three personas, and each milestone is a separate checkable claim, so a failure anywhere is a finding, not a wasted week.

2) Scope and non-goals

In scope: M1 to M8 as written in the brief, on the suggested tickers ARCC, OBDC, FSK, GBDC, MAIN, TSLX, HTGC, PSEC, 8 quarters of 10-K and 10-Q.

Non-goals (from the brief, each with the reason): - Auth, accounts, multi-tenancy. One user, run locally. Nothing to protect. - Scheduler or automated refresh. The cache is the data version; refresh is make data. - More than 8 BDCs. Coverage is a stated limitation; parse quality per filer beats breadth in two days. - Any fine-tuned model. Can't be evaluated with this little labelled data. - Deployment beyond docker compose up. Nobody is on call for this. - Sponsor identity. Not in BDC filings. Future enrichment.

Trade-offs accepted: heuristic non-accrual detection with an LLM fallback and a non_accrual_source column; an eight-filer universe that biases comparables toward upper-middle-market direct lending, which the coverage banner says.

3) Expected impact

If this ships, an underwriter gets a spread distribution with n and the rows behind it instead of a broker's anecdote, and a monitoring analyst gets 15 names with reasons and a measured precision instead of a gut list. If it doesn't, those decisions stay undefended. We don't know the size of the impact. To find out: put screen 1 in front of two underwriters after M8 and count who'd bring it to committee.

4) AI behaviour contract (M7 agent)

Dimension Specification
Primary task retrieve-and-answer over the tape, the model and the graph
Inputs four tools: get_comparables, get_borrower, sponsor_or_colender_exposure, review_list; no free web access
Constraints every numeric claim carries the accession number it came from; answers state "data as of"
Disallowed inventing a number, a borrower, a sponsor, or an accession; answering outside the tape's coverage
Model Gemini via langchain-google-genai, existing key, no fine-tune
Tracing LangSmith if available, else MLflow tracing; every inference inspectable

The rule: cite or refuse. No accession, no number. The agent says it doesn't know and why. Refusing is a correct answer; guessing is a failure.

What counts as a citation (M7.2): a single position cites its accession; an aggregate cites the accession list of the rows it used, with n; a model output cites the MLflow run id plus the accessions of the input rows. Anything else is uncited.

5) Success

Success is team/criteria.json. No number here that isn't in the brief.

  • Gated: the 28 ids marked gated: true, M1.1 to M8.4 and D1 to D3. They are the brief's acceptance tests.
  • Product, non-gated: P1 (underwriter), P2 (monitoring analyst), P3 (allocator), one per JTBD. A failure is a README finding with a written reason, not a block.
  • Passing bar: every gated criterion PASS on observed output.

Guardrails: M3.2 precision >= 0.95 is a floor; recall is reported whatever it is (M3.3). If the model doesn't beat the baseline, we keep the result and say so (M5.3).

Kill criteria (the README's question 5, decided now): - The tape is abandoned if M2.2 (parse audit >= 90%) still fails after the protocol's three loops, or a filer's SOI can't be parsed and no replacement filer gets the cache back to M1.1. - The cross-filer story is dropped, not the project, if M3.2 (precision >= 0.95) is unreachable after three loops: dispersion features and P3 go, and the README says the tape is single-filer. - A model that doesn't beat the baseline is not a kill condition. The baseline ships and M5.3 says why.
