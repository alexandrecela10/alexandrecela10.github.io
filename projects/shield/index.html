Shield

Reads a capital project's documents, finds claims exposure and contract notice deadlines, and drafts the notice for a named person to approve.

Infrastructure equityAsset managementPrototypeSynthetic data

Problem

Contractor claims and missed contract notice deadlines move value from the project company to the contractor, and the fund's asset manager first sees the loss in the quarterly valuation.

For

PlayerInfrastructure fund holding equity in a project company that runs a large capital project
UserAsset manager: decides which commercial events to escalate to the project company, and whether a drafted notice is approved for issue
BuyerFund partner or head of asset management: decides whether to fund the tool across the portfolio, and what goes into the quarterly valuation

User stories

  1. Deadline. As an asset manager, I want each open notice deadline with the documents behind it, so the project company acts before the window closes.
  2. Equity at risk. As an asset manager, I want each event's value at risk in fund equity terms, so I tell the partner before the quarterly valuation.
  3. Control. As a fund partner, I want every notice approved by a named person and logged, so the fund can show who decided what.

Try it

  1. Pick a detected event. EV-1 has 6 days left on the project company's notice window.
  2. Follow the evidence chain: letter, site record, schedule entry, contract clause, deadline countdown. Read the fund equity at risk below it.
  3. Review the draft notice, then approve or reject it. The decision lands in the log. Switch "Acting as" to the analyst to see the gate refuse.

Sample data only. Fictional fund, project company, project and contractor. "Today" is fixed at 26 Aug 2026. The page runs in the browser with no server and no model call. Agent outputs were computed offline and frozen into the page.

Details

Value equation

value = (outcome x likelihood) / (time delay x effort & sacrifice)

ASSET MANAGER

Outcome
"I hear about a claim or a closing notice window weeks before quarter end, with the documents, and the project company files on time."
Likelihood
MEDIUM. Every deadline and quote can be checked. The detector found 3 of 4 events on the eval set. No asset manager has tested it.
Delay
LOW. It runs on documents the project company already holds. Time to first alert is unmeasured.
Effort
MEDIUM. Needs read access to the project company's document and schedule exports. Each draft needs a review.
Sacrifice
MEDIUM. Project company management may read it as the fund checking their work. A register of known exposures can be disclosed in a dispute.

FUND PARTNER

Outcome
"No write-down from a claim we could have seen. Each valuation cut traces to a document."
Likelihood
LOW. The link from earlier notices to fewer valuation surprises is a hypothesis. No portfolio data.
Delay
HIGH. The effect on valuations shows over quarters.
Effort
MEDIUM. Document access depends on the fund's information rights in each asset.
Sacrifice
MEDIUM. Some goodwill with project company management. Legal review of the exposure register.

Verdict: outcome is clear for both. The partner's likelihood and delay are weakest.

  • Fix 1, backtest: run it on one closed project's archive. Count the valuation write-downs from commercial events, and check whether the documents showed each one earlier. This gives evidence in weeks, not quarters.
  • Fix 2, sacrifice: run read-only, with the project company's counsel as owner of the register. Disclosure risk then sits with the party that already holds the documents.
Sub-problems

"Commercial event" here means a fact in a document that can start a contract notice period or a claim.

  1. Detection. Claim signals sit in letters, minutes and daily reports that nobody reads in full.
  2. Evidence. The link from a letter to its site record, schedule entry and clause is assembled by hand.
  3. Deadline. Notice periods depend on the clause and its amendments. A missed window can waive the entitlement.
  4. Equity view. Event cost is stated in project dollars. The fund reads value in equity terms.
  5. Control. A drafted notice must not go out without a named person's approval.
  6. Quantum. What a claim is worth needs a quantity surveyor's assessment.
  7. Reporting. Events need to reach the quarterly valuation and the LP report.
  8. Portfolio coverage. Each asset has its own contract form, amendments and document systems.

This build tackles 1 to 5. 1, 2 and 4 are partial.

How it works
  1. Ingest: documents in. Documents from another project are refused and logged.
  2. Detect: each document is scanned for trigger phrases. Each hit gets a clause and a confidence. Below 0.6, it is not surfaced.
  3. Verify: each quote must appear word for word in its source document, or the event is downgraded.
  4. Deadline: trigger date plus the clause's notice period. A missing amendment makes the date provisional.
  5. Value (demo): delay beyond float times daily cost, plus direct cost. The amount above remaining contingency, times the fund's share, is equity at risk.
  6. Draft: a notice citing each source, with status PENDING_APPROVAL.
  7. Gate: only a registered approver can approve. Every approval, rejection and refusal is logged. Nothing is sent.
Hypotheses, one per tackled sub-problem

1 · DETECTION

Before -> after
event found when the formal claim arrives or in the quarterly pack -> flagged on the date of the document that shows it
Hypothesis
If every letter, minute and daily report is scanned against clause triggers, then the asset manager learns of an event sooner, because the signal is in the documents before the formal claim.
Metric
Days from triggering document to asset manager awareness. Down. Baseline unobserved. Guardrail: precision at or above 0.85 (eval: 1.00 on 8 documents).
Test
Backtest on one closed project's archive against an expert-labelled event list.

2 · EVIDENCE

Before -> after
chain assembled by hand -> each link quote-checked against its source
Hypothesis
If each event arrives with verified links from letter to clause, then review time per event goes down, because the reviewer checks links instead of searching for them.
Metric
Minutes to review one event; share of chains needing correction. Down. Baseline unobserved.
Test
Two reviewers, 10 events, with and without the chain.

3 · DEADLINE

Before -> after
periods tracked from memory or a spreadsheet -> computed from clause and amendments, provisional if one is missing
Hypothesis
If deadlines come from the clause and its amendment chain, then fewer windows close without a notice, because errors like 28 days vs an amended 21 are caught.
Metric
Notice windows closed without a notice, per project-year. Down. Baseline unobserved.
Test
Recompute deadlines on a closed project and compare with the notices actually filed.

4 · EQUITY VIEW

Before -> after
project dollars, seen in the quarterly pack -> fund equity at risk per event, inputs on the page
Hypothesis
If the asset manager sees equity at risk when an event is detected, then fewer valuation moves reach the partner unannounced, because the partner hears before the valuation date.
Metric
Share of commercial-event valuation moves flagged before quarter end. Up. Baseline unobserved.
Test
Walk the last 4 quarters of one asset. For each write-down, was there an earlier document trail?

5 · CONTROL

Before -> after
drafts by email, approver unclear -> registered approver, logged decision, no send function
Hypothesis
If only registered approvers can approve and every decision is logged, then no notice leaves without an owner, because the system can't send and refuses anyone else.
Metric
Notices issued without a logged approver. Down to zero. Eval: 3 of 3 notices stopped at PENDING_APPROVAL.
Test
Governance evals, plus attempts to approve as a non-approver.

Observed in the build
  • Eval run (2026-10-01, 8 synthetic documents): 17 of 17 evals passed. Precision 1.00 (3 of 3), recall 0.75 (3 of 4, missed LTR-006), 2 of 2 non-events suppressed. Supports 1, with a recall gap.
  • Evidence: 3 of 3 surfaced citations verified. The adversarial adapter emitted 7 fabricated citations; 0 surfaced. Supports 2.
  • Deadlines: 3 of 3 match the golden dates, incl. 1 flagged provisional for a missing amendment. Supports 3.
  • Governance: 1 out-of-scope document refused. 3 of 3 notices stopped at PENDING_APPROVAL. An unauthorised approver was refused. 21 audit events, hash chain intact. Supports 5.
  • Demo tests: 10 of 10 pass (node --test demo/tests/calc.test.js). They cover deadlines, amendments, status boundaries, parity with the agent's 3 deadlines, 15 chain quotes and value at risk.
  • Demo figures: EV-1 shows $19.58M project exposure and $6.63M fund equity at risk. All 3 events total $9.34M, 3.9% of stake value. Inputs are synthetic, so these show the mechanism, not a result. Relates to 4.
Limits and trade-offs
  • Data: synthetic. 8 eval documents. The demo shows 15 documents, 2 of them added for the demo.
  • Users: no asset manager or partner has used it. Every "before" is assumed.
  • Detection: keyword rules, not a model. Accuracy on real correspondence is unmeasured.
  • Awareness date: the agent uses the document date. Contracts often use "became aware, or should have", which can be earlier.
  • Value: assumes every open position is lost, and that equity funds overruns above contingency. That overstates expected loss.
  • Who approves: the demo makes the asset manager the approver. In practice the project company issues notices, and the fund acts through its shareholder rights.
  • Source mismatch: EV-1 in the demo uses the walkthrough record (21-day amended bar). The agent's eval version of the same event uses 28 days.
