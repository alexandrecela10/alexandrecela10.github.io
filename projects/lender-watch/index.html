01 · VISION

A company name gets matched once, and everyone reuses the answer.

Today, every team matches company names by hand, again each quarter. Instead, a person confirms a match once, with the source as proof, and the answer reaches you in your workbook.

02 · THE PROBLEM

4 things go wrong today.

1 · You can't find the loans

172 lenders publish, each in its own wording. One company, KnowBe4, sits with 10 funds under 9 different descriptions.

2 · A wrong match looks foolish

Searching "Ascend" also finds Ascend Learning, a different business. Ask a fund manager about that loan and you've questioned the wrong company.

3 · You hear late

Lenders publish about 6 weeks after a quarter ends. Fund reports take 2 to 4 months, and they give the owner's view, not the lender's.

4 · The fix isn't where you work

A spreadsheet lookup needs identical text, so it found 0 of 5 names in our sample tabs. Nothing tells you why.

03 · THE SOLUTION

Code matches the clear names. You settle the unclear ones. No AI ever produces a number.

What it is

A quarterly check of what lenders think your companies' loans are worth.

What it does

Finds each company's loans, compares values across lenders and quarters, and flags debt that's been marked down.

What you get

Your spreadsheet back, with a flag per company and a link to the filing for each loan.

You reach it from your workbook, three ways.

You know the tool

You type a formula, or tell Claude to run it. Results fill the sheet beside your list.

You know the problem

You ask "how would I reconcile these companies?" You get the steps, the limits and an offer to run it.

You know neither

Your lookup between two tabs returns #N/A. The assistant says why and offers the fix, without being asked.

Exabeam appears in filings only as LogRhythm, so here you see its loans at 86.2% of the amount owed. A lookup returns #N/A. A looser match would link "Foundation" to Foundation Risk Partners, a different company.

The Exabeam link is an AI check no person has confirmed.

The AI understands the question. Code does the maths. You decide what's unclear.

06 · LIMITATIONS

What this doesn't prove yet.

  • Users: no investor has seen it. The need is inferred from public sources.
  • Excel: an add-in with =ERM.RECONCILE() and a web connector for Claude in Excel are built, and tested with a stand-in for Excel. Neither has run in real Excel or Claude in Excel.
  • The offer: a code rule spots the failed lookup, so it only catches what that rule covers.
  • Accuracy: an AI wrote the test answers and an AI does the judging, so the 100% isn't independent. The test covers 84 companies.
  • Prediction: two quarters can't show a flag leads to anything. 9 of the 14 flagged were already below 90 cents a quarter earlier.
  • Coverage: banks don't publish loans, so "not flagged" only covers lenders that do. Only 8 of 59 companies from one firm show up.
  • Timing: the regulator's data updates monthly. We couldn't confirm the exact date.
  • Reuse: any list of names works. No second data source has been added.
  • Shared copies: two people on one local copy can overwrite each other's AI setting.
  • Legal: no lawyer has reviewed it. This isn't investment advice.

How it's built what goes in, what happens, what comes out
  1. In: your list of names, and the regulator's tables of holdings.
  2. Clean: keep current loans. Leave out rows whose amounts don't add up, and count them.
  3. Search: look for name forms, spacing differences and rare first words.
  4. Decide: your saved answer first, then an exact-name rule, then the AI if it's on. Anything unclear goes to you.
  5. Compare: pair each loan with last quarter, then average by loan size, so bigger loans count more.
  6. Out: a spreadsheet, a review app, a workbook panel or a connector for AI apps. Every value links to its filing.

Terms on this page plain definitions
  • Private equity fund: a pool of investors' money that buys whole companies.
  • Fund manager: the firm that runs the fund and owns the companies.
  • Listed lender (a BDC): a stock-market-listed lender that must publish its loans.
  • Flag: on average, lenders value the debt below 90 cents per dollar owed, or that average fell 5 cents or more in a quarter.
  • Answer key: name pairs labelled in advance, used to score each method.
  • Lookup: a spreadsheet formula, such as XLOOKUP, that finds a row by identical text.
  • Connector: a way for an AI app such as Claude to call a tested tool.

In the working papers below, LP means an investor in funds, GP the fund manager, and par the amount owed.

Vision

Every list of company names in private capital links to one company record, with the source row as proof. Today each team matches names by hand, inside each workflow, and starts again every quarter. In this vision a name is matched once, a person confirms it once, and every later workflow reuses the answer.

  • First: LP analysts see which companies in their funds their lenders have marked down.
  • Next, same engine:
  • Secondaries buyers find which companies overlap across the funds they price.
  • Lenders track one borrower across lenders and loans.
  • Data teams check a vendor's file against their own.
  • Delivery: a spreadsheet, a review app, or a tool AI agents call.

Analysts' time moves from matching names to judging what the data means.

Problem Statement

LP analysts can't see which companies in their PE funds their lenders already value below par. The loan values are public, but no one matches them to the LP's list of companies.

Current Situation

BDCs, the listed lenders, file every loan and its value about 42 days after quarter end. SEC adds them to its monthly data sets, so about day 62, which is what we read today. GP reports are due 60 to 120 days after. Using the earlier data means finding each company by hand across about 160 filers.

A concrete case. Vista owns KnowBe4. Ten BDC funds hold its loans, described 9 different ways. Golub Capital Private Credit Fund valued its loan at 78.5% of par on 30 June 2026, down from 89.7%. All 9 funds with a usable value sit between 77.5% and 78.5%, so lenders agree. It filed on 11 August. An LP matching names sees this on 11 August. An LP waiting for Vista's report gets it by 29 August at best, and that report shows Vista's equity value, not the lenders' view.

Proposed Solution

An entity reconciliation engine for private capital. It takes the LP's list of company names as written and:

  1. Finds every BDC loan to each company, across all filers and quarters.
  2. Matches with proof. An exact-name rule settles clear names. A language model (an AI that reads text) compares name, sector and loan terms on the rest and writes its reason. A person confirms the rest once, and the answer is saved.
  3. Compares each lender's value against last quarter and against other lenders, loan by loan, each value linked to its filing row.

Recommendation

Go, scoped to a 2-week build of the matching engine, with the LP read-through as its first use, measured against an answer key. The stress data is already sold by at least 7 vendors: 9fin flags loans below 90, and PitchBook links BDC loans to company profiles. Matching a client's own messy list to those loans, with proof, is the part not sold off the shelf, and it carries over to other lists and sources. The evidence for that is weaker than first reported: plain matching already gets 94% of loans right. The harder work is companies borrowing under other names and bad filer data. What we're building

Lender Watch is a quarterly check for LP and fund-of-funds analysts.

  • Goes in: a spreadsheet of company names, written the way the LP writes them.
  • Comes out: the same spreadsheet plus, per company, each BDC lender's loan value this quarter and last, flags, and a link to each filing row.
  • Gain: the analyst sees which companies their lenders have marked down about day 62 after quarter end, as built. GP reports are due day 60 to 120, so the lead is up to about 2 months, and none against the earliest reports.
  • Can't do yet: cover lenders that don't file publicly, read filings the day they land (day 42), read GP PDFs, compare with GP equity values, or say whether a flag predicts anything.

Matching doesn't depend on the source, and any list of names works. No second source has been added, so reuse across sources is untested.

Values without a filing link (guardrail) n/a 0

Kill criteria - 3 LP analysts say PitchBook or 9fin already tells them which of their companies are stressed: stop the commercial case, keep the engine for side projects. - Precision stays below 97% after 2 weeks: no flags go to users, and we rework matching.

Rollout

  • Week 1: clean the data, build the answer key, compare 3 matching approaches. Pass: one approach reaches ≥ 97% precision.
  • Week 2: spreadsheet in and out, review app, agent tool, flags. Pass: all metrics above met on the 291-company list.
  • Then: 2 or 3 LP analysts test it on their own list, on their machine. The AI judge stays off unless they turn it on, because it sends names to Anthropic. Pass: each names a past decision a flag would have changed.

Rollback: if flag confirmation falls below 90%, show values and links only, without flags.

Name cleaning plus fuzzy match The baseline: 94.4% loan precision
