Alpha Scout

Describe the startup you want. Get a scored shortlist from the web, every score tied to a quote and a link.

Problem

The best early-stage deals often never pitch a fund. A fund that waits for inbound sees them after faster funds do.

For

PlayerEarly-stage VC fund investing in MENA
UserInvestment analyst: decides which companies the fund contacts before they pitch
BuyerVC CEO: decides where analyst time goes and answers to LPs on pipeline quality

User stories

  1. As an investment analyst, I want startups similar to a portfolio winner found on sources I trust, so that I see them before they pitch anyone.
  2. As an investment analyst, I want each candidate's stage, size and region checked against its own website, LinkedIn and funding news, so that I only review companies the fund can invest in.
  3. As a VC CEO, I want every score to show its quote and source link, so that the outbound pipeline holds up with IC and LPs.

Try it

  1. Open Load Previous Search in the sidebar and load a saved example. It costs nothing.
  2. Read the comparison table and the 2x2 matrix, then open a company to check each score against its quote and link.
  3. Pick a fictional portfolio seed, adjust the filters and run a live search. 2 per visit, up to 5 companies each.

Portfolio seeds are fictional. Results name real companies found in public sources. Live searches use a shared free-tier budget that resets daily.

value = (outcome x likelihood) / (time delay x effort & sacrifice)

MEDIUM. Every score carries a quote and a link to check. Search quality depends on the sources chosen. No analyst has measured hit rate
Delay
LOW. A 10-company search took a few minutes, end to end (observed on 2 searches, not timed)

LOW. The analyst still decides who to contact. Gives up: a scored view they didn't build themselves

Outcome
first fund in the room on the best companies in its thesis, with a pipeline it can show LPs
Likelihood
LOW-MEDIUM. No data yet on whether outbound finds lead to deals
Delay
HIGH. Deal outcomes show over quarters
Effort

Sacrifice
MEDIUM. Search queries describing the thesis go to third-party search and AI providers

Verdict: likelihood is the weakest factor for both. Cheapest fix: track 3 months of outbound shortlists and count how many become first calls. The CEO's sacrifice is addressed with a self-hosted model, listed under non-goals.

Sub-problems
  1. Discovery. Analysts find startups through inbound and their network. Companies outside both are missed.
  2. Eligibility. Stage, size and region claims in articles are often wrong or out of date.
  3. Evidence. AI-written company summaries can invent facts.
  4. Comparison. Candidates found in different places can't be compared side by side.
  5. Repeat work. The same ineligible companies come back in every search.
  6. Outreach. Getting from a shortlist to a first call is manual.

This build tackles 1 to 5. Outreach is a non-goal.

How it works
  1. A seed profile (problem, clients, industry, technology, region, size) becomes one search query, run only on the sources the analyst selects. Defaults: MENAbytes, Wamda, MAGNiTT, ArabNet, Crunchbase MENA.
  2. The language model extracts candidate companies from the results. A company name must appear in its source text, or it's dropped.
  3. Three enrichment steps run per candidate: website check, LinkedIn page, and funding stage from news.
  4. Filters remove companies over the employee cap, outside MENA, or past the latest stage allowed.
  5. Each remaining company is scored 1 to 5 on offer power, sales ability, tech moat and founder strength. Each score has a quote and a source link, and the page shows whether the quote was found in the source.
  6. A reviewer step checks the results. Companies that failed a filter go on a blacklist, so later searches skip them.
Hypotheses, one per tackled sub-problem

1 · DISCOVERY

Before -> after
inbound and network only -> similar startups found on chosen sources
Hypothesis
If analysts search from a portfolio winner's profile, then they find eligible companies they didn't know, because the search covers sources they don't read daily.
Metric
Share of shortlisted companies new to the fund's CRM. Up. Baseline unobserved. Guardrail: share later judged off-thesis stays flat.
Test
Run 10 searches, check each result against the CRM.

2 · ELIGIBILITY

Before -> after
stage and size taken from the article -> checked against website, LinkedIn and funding news
Hypothesis
If each candidate is checked against three sources, then fewer ineligible companies reach the analyst, because one article is often out of date.
Metric
Share of shortlisted companies an analyst marks ineligible. Down. Baseline unobserved.
Test
Analyst labels eligibility on 2 searches, with and without enrichment.

3 · EVIDENCE

Before -> after
AI summary taken on trust -> quote and link per score, quote checked against source
Hypothesis
If every score shows a checked quote, then analysts verify faster and trust the shortlist more, because they check one quote instead of reading the article.
Metric
Share of scores whose quote is found in the source. Up. Measured per company on the page; not yet summarised.
Test
Count grounded vs ungrounded quotes across the 2 saved searches.

4 · COMPARISON

Before -> after
notes in different places -> one table and a 2x2 matrix on the same 4 dimensions
Hypothesis
If candidates are scored on the same 4 dimensions, then two analysts agree more on who to contact first, because they compare like with like.
Metric
Top-3 overlap between two analysts on the same search. Up. Baseline unobserved.
Test
Two analysts rank one saved search, with and without scores.

5 · REPEAT WORK

Before -> after
same ineligible companies every search -> blacklisted and skipped
Hypothesis
If ineligible companies are blacklisted, then later searches cost fewer API calls per eligible company, because known misses are skipped before enrichment.
Metric
API calls per eligible company over repeated searches. Down. Baseline unobserved.
Test
Run the same seed 3 times and compare call counts.

Observed in the build
  • Saved examples: 2 searches on fictional seeds, 10 candidates requested each. 4 companies passed the filters and were scored in each.
  • Model change: gemini-2.5 models returned "no longer available to new users" on a new key. The app now uses gemini-3.8-flash.
  • Tests: 4 demo tests on the cost meter, the per-visitor isolation and the fictional seeds.
Limits and trade-offs
  • Users: no analyst has measured hit rate. Every "before" is assumed.
  • Coverage: results depend on what the chosen sources have published. Some sources block page fetches (403), so those companies get less evidence.
  • Budget: the free search plan covers about 15 live searches a month. The saved examples carry the demo past that.
