Alpha Scout

Describe the kind of startup a fund wants to back. Get a scored shortlist from the web, each score backed by a quote and a link.

Problem

The best young companies often never email a startup fund asking for money. A fund that waits for founders to come to it meets them after faster funds do.

Example: a company that fits the fund gets written up on a regional startup news site. Nobody at the fund reads that article, so a rival fund calls the founders first.

For

PlayerA startup investment fund (venture capital) backing young companies in the Middle East and North Africa (MENA)
UserA junior investor at the fund: decides which companies to contact before those companies ask for money
BuyerThe head of the fund: decides where the junior investors spend their time, and reports to the fund's own investors on the quality of companies in view

Terms on this page

  • Venture capital (VC): money invested in young companies for a share of them.
  • Early-stage: a company at its first funding rounds (seed, Series A).
  • Portfolio company: a company the fund already owns a share of.
  • Thesis: the kinds of companies a fund chooses to back.
  • Inbound / outbound: founders contact the fund / the fund contacts founders.
  • Eligible: right size, region and funding stage for the fund.
  • CRM: the fund's database of companies and contacts.
  • Quote check: confirms a quote really appears in its source.

User stories

  1. As a junior investor, I want startups like one of our best portfolio companies found on news sites I trust, so that I see them before they ask anyone for money.
  2. As a junior investor, I want each company's funding stage, size and region checked against its own website, LinkedIn page and funding news, so that I only review companies the fund is allowed to invest in.
  3. As the head of the fund, I want every score to show its quote and source link, so that the list of companies we approach holds up in front of the investment committee and the fund's own investors.

Try it

  1. Open Load Previous Search in the sidebar and load a saved example. It costs nothing.
  2. Read the comparison table and the 2x2 matrix. Then open a company and check each score against its quote and link.
  3. Pick a fictional model company (the app calls it a portfolio seed), adjust the filters and run a live search. 2 per visit, up to 5 companies each.

The model companies are fictional. Results name real companies found in public sources. Live searches share a free allowance that resets daily.

Would they use it? (value equation)

value = (what they get x chance it works) / (wait x effort & what they give up)

What they get

Chance it works
LOW-MEDIUM. No data yet on whether companies found this way lead to investments
Wait
HIGH. Investment results take quarters to show

What they give up
MEDIUM. Searches that describe what the fund wants go to outside search and AI companies

Verdict: chance it works is the weakest factor for both. Cheapest fix: track 3 months of shortlists and count how many lead to a first meeting with founders. The head of the fund's data concern is solved by running the AI model in-house, listed under non-goals.

Sub-problems
  1. Discovery. Junior investors find startups through founders who contact the fund, and through people they know. Companies outside both are missed.
  2. Eligibility. Funding stage, size and region in news articles are often wrong or out of date.
  3. Evidence. AI-written company summaries can invent facts.
  4. Comparison. Companies found in different places can't be compared side by side.
  5. Repeat work. The same ineligible companies come back in every search.
  6. Outreach. Getting from a shortlist to a first meeting is done by hand.

This build tackles 1 to 5. Outreach is a non-goal.

How it works
  1. A model company profile (problem, clients, industry, technology, region, size) becomes one search, run only on the sources the investor selects. Defaults: MENAbytes, Wamda, MAGNiTT, ArabNet, Crunchbase MENA.
  2. An AI model pulls company names from the results. A company name must appear in its source text, or it's dropped.
  3. Three extra checks run per company: its website, its LinkedIn page, and its funding stage from news.
  4. Filters remove companies with too many employees, outside MENA, or past the latest funding stage allowed.
  5. Each remaining company is scored 1 to 5 on how strong its offer is, how well it sells, how hard its technology is to copy, and how strong its founders are. Each score has a quote and a source link. The page shows whether the quote was found in the source.
  6. A review step checks the results. Companies that failed a filter go on a skip list, so later searches ignore them.
Hypotheses, one per tackled sub-problem

1 · DISCOVERY

Before -> after
founders who contact the fund and personal contacts only -> similar startups found on chosen sources
Hypothesis
If investors search from the profile of one of their best portfolio companies, then they find eligible companies they didn't know, because the search covers sources they don't read daily.
Metric
Share of shortlisted companies not yet in the fund's CRM. Up. Baseline unobserved. Guardrail: share later judged outside the fund's thesis stays flat.
Test
Run 10 searches, check each result against the CRM.

2 · ELIGIBILITY

Before -> after
stage and size taken from the article -> checked against website, LinkedIn and funding news
Hypothesis
If each company is checked against three sources, then fewer ineligible companies reach the investor, because one article is often out of date.
Metric
Share of shortlisted companies an investor marks ineligible. Down. Baseline unobserved.
Test
An investor labels eligibility on 2 searches, with and without the extra checks.

3 · EVIDENCE

Before -> after
AI summary taken on trust -> quote and link per score, quote checked against source
Hypothesis
If every score shows a checked quote, then investors verify faster and trust the shortlist more, because they check one quote instead of reading the article.
Metric
Share of scores whose quote is found in the source. Up. Measured per company on the page; not yet added up.
Test
Count quotes found vs not found in their source across the 2 saved searches.

4 · COMPARISON

Before -> after
notes in different places -> one table and a 2x2 matrix on the same 4 criteria
Hypothesis
If companies are scored on the same 4 criteria, then two investors agree more on who to contact first, because they compare like with like.
Metric
How many of two investors' top-3 picks match on the same search. Up. Baseline unobserved.
Test
Two investors rank one saved search, with and without scores.

5 · REPEAT WORK

Before -> after
same ineligible companies every search -> put on a skip list and ignored
Hypothesis
If ineligible companies go on a skip list, then later searches make fewer paid API calls (requests to outside services) per eligible company, because known misses are skipped before the extra checks.
Metric
API calls per eligible company over repeated searches. Down. Baseline unobserved.
Test
Run the same model company 3 times and compare call counts.

Observed in the build
  • Saved examples: 2 searches on fictional model companies, 10 companies requested each. 4 companies passed the filters and were scored in each.
  • Model change: gemini-2.5 models returned "no longer available to new users" on a new key. The app now uses gemini-3.8-flash.
  • Tests: 4 demo tests on the cost meter, keeping each visitor's session separate, and the fictional model companies.
Limits and trade-offs
  • Users: no investor has measured how many results turn out useful. Every "before" is assumed.
  • Coverage: results depend on what the chosen sources have published. Some sources block page downloads (403), so those companies get less evidence.
  • Budget: the free search plan covers about 15 live searches a month. The saved examples keep the demo working past that.
