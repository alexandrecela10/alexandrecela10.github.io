01 · VISION

Your fund meets the best founders first.

A fund that waits for founders to come to it meets them after faster funds do. The vision: you search from the profile of one of your best portfolio companies, on the sources you trust, and every company on the shortlist comes with checked evidence.

02 · THE PROBLEM

Five things go wrong today.

1 · You miss companies outside your network

You find startups through founders who contact the fund, and through people you know.

2 · You review companies you can't back

Funding stage, size and region in news articles are often wrong or out of date.

3 · You can't trust the summary

AI-written company summaries can invent facts.

4 · You can't compare what you found

Companies found in different places have no common scores.

5 · You pay for the same misses twice

The same ineligible companies come back in every search.

03 · THE SOLUTION

The AI searches and scores. Code checks the quotes. You decide who to contact.

  1. Search from a model company. Its profile becomes one search, run only on the sources you select.
  2. Keep only real names. A company name must appear in its source text, or it's dropped.
  3. Check eligibility three ways. Website, LinkedIn and funding news. Filters remove companies too large, outside MENA or past your stage.
  4. Score with evidence. Offer, sales, technology and founders, 1 to 5, each with a quote, a link and whether the quote was found.
  5. Skip known misses. Companies that fail a filter go on a skip list for later searches.

05 · THE VALUE

What it's worth, and what it asks of you.

A company that fits your fund gets written up on a regional startup news site. Nobody at your fund reads that article, so a rival fund calls the founders first.

You, the junior investor
You get
A shortlist of eligible startups that haven't asked for money yet, each with the evidence behind its score.
Will you believe it?
Medium. Every score carries a quote and a link to check. Search quality depends on the sources chosen.
How fast
Low delay. A 10-company search took a few minutes, start to finish (seen on 2 searches, not timed).
What it takes
Low. Pick a model company and run. Filters and sources can be edited.
What you give up
Low. You still decide who to contact. You give up building the scored view yourself.
The head of your fund
They get
The first fund to meet the best companies it wants to back, with a list it can show its own investors.
Will they believe it?
Low to medium. No data yet on whether companies found this way lead to investments.
How fast
High delay. Investment results take quarters to show.
What it takes
Medium. Set up API keys (access codes for outside services), and the team agrees on scoring criteria once.
What they give up
Medium. Searches that describe what the fund wants go to outside search and AI companies.

The weak link is belief, for both. The cheapest fix: track 3 months of shortlists and count how many lead to a first meeting with founders.

06 · LIMITATIONS

What this doesn't prove yet.

  • Users: no investor has measured how many results turn out useful. Every "before" is assumed.
  • Coverage: results depend on what the chosen sources have published. Some sources block page downloads (403), so those companies get less evidence.
  • Budget: the free search plan covers about 15 live searches a month. The saved examples keep the demo working past that.

All sub-problems the 6 found, and the 5 this build tackles
  1. Discovery. Junior investors find startups through founders who contact the fund, and through people they know. Companies outside both are missed.
  2. Eligibility. Funding stage, size and region in news articles are often wrong or out of date.
  3. Evidence. AI-written company summaries can invent facts.
  4. Comparison. Companies found in different places can't be compared side by side.
  5. Repeat work. The same ineligible companies come back in every search.
  6. Outreach. Getting from a shortlist to a first meeting is done by hand.

This build tackles 1 to 5. Outreach is a non-goal.

How it's built search, name check, extra checks, filters, scoring, review
  1. A model company profile (problem, clients, industry, technology, region, size) becomes one search, run only on the sources the investor selects. Defaults: MENAbytes, Wamda, MAGNiTT, ArabNet, Crunchbase MENA.
  2. An AI model pulls company names from the results. A company name must appear in its source text, or it's dropped.
  3. Three extra checks run per company: its website, its LinkedIn page, and its funding stage from news.
  4. Filters remove companies with too many employees, outside MENA, or past the latest funding stage allowed.
  5. Each remaining company is scored 1 to 5 on how strong its offer is, how well it sells, how hard its technology is to copy, and how strong its founders are. Each score has a quote and a source link. The page shows whether the quote was found in the source.
  6. A review step checks the results. Companies that failed a filter go on a skip list, so later searches ignore them.

Terms on this page plain definitions
  • Venture capital (VC): money invested in young companies for a share of them.
  • Early-stage: a company at its first funding rounds (seed, Series A).
  • Portfolio company: a company the fund already owns a share of.
  • Thesis: the kinds of companies a fund chooses to back.
  • Inbound / outbound: founders contact the fund / the fund contacts founders.
  • Eligible: right size, region and funding stage for the fund.
  • CRM: the fund's database of companies and contacts.
  • Quote check: confirms a quote really appears in its source.
