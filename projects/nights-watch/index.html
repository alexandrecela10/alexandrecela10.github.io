01 · VISION

No missed interest payment arrives without a warning.

Today you learn a borrower is short of cash on the payment date, when the easy fixes are gone. The vision: every borrower's usable cash forecast to its next payment date, ranked by money at stake, with every number one click from its source.

02 · THE PROBLEM

Five things go wrong today.

1 · A borrower looks safe when it isn't

The cash total you watch includes currencies the borrower can't convert or send. In the sample file, $9.31M is held and $796K can pay you.

2 · You see the shortfall too late

Cash on the payment date is unknown today, and a single guess hides the bad case.

3 · Big losses wait behind small ones

Each analyst handles borrowers in their own order, not by money at stake and recoverability.

4 · Warnings get rebuilt or ignored

A warning you can't check gets rebuilt in a spreadsheet, or set aside.

5 · No history to set the threshold

Actions and outcomes aren't recorded, so nobody can tune when a warning should fire.

03 · THE SOLUTION

Code forecasts usable cash. You decide what to do.

  1. Only usable cash counts. Each balance is converted to dollars and weighted by how convertible its currency is: 1 if free, 0.5 if partly, 0 if blocked.
  2. A range on the payment date. Low, lower-middle and middle guesses of usable cash (P10, P40, P50) set the level: None, Low, Medium or High. Bank data older than 3 days raises it.
  3. Worst first, then money at stake. You get a ranked list with P1 to P4 priority, split by principal and recoverability.
  4. Every number traced, every action logged. Each figure shows its formula and source field. Your action is logged next to the warning.

05 · THE VALUE

What it's worth, and what it asks of you.

In the sample bank file, a borrower holds $9.31M. Only $796K is in currencies it can use to pay you.

You, the credit analyst
You get
"I open the list and the borrower most likely to miss its interest payment, with the most money at stake, is on top. I can show why in one click."
Will you believe it?
Medium. Every number is traced. The forecast assumes cash falls by the same amount each day. It hasn't been tested against real missed payments.
How fast
Low delay. The demo recalculates every borrower when bank data changes. Time from real bank data to warning is unobserved: no live connection yet.
What it takes
Medium. No spreadsheet to rebuild. The CRM must stay up to date: next payment date, cash or PIK interest, recoverability.
What you give up
Medium. Your own order of work, for a shared rule. One more screen next to the CRM.
Your head of credit
They get
"No borrower defaults without us seeing it coming, and the team works on the most money at risk each day."
Will they believe it?
Low to medium. Depends on how many borrowers' bank data is connected, how complete the CRM is, and a threshold set on few known missed payments.
How fast
High delay. Missed payments are rare, so recall takes quarters to measure.
What it takes
High. Bank and CRM connectors, data quality checks, access control and a data protection review.
What they give up
Medium. Borrower bank data sits in one more system. Catching every miss first means accepting more false alarms.

The weak link is belief, for both of you. For you: test the forecast on past data. If actual cash on the payment date lands above P10 about 90% of the time, the forecast is well set. For your head of credit: sit with two analysts and compare their order of work with the tool's list on the same day.

06 · LIMITATIONS

What this doesn't prove yet.

  • Data: the borrowers are made up. The sample bank file is one snapshot in time, so spending rate and months of cash left can't be computed from it.
  • Users: untested. Every "before" is assumed.
  • Accuracy: unmeasured. The forecast assumes cash falls by the same amount each day, with bell-curve swings.
  • Discounts: counting partly convertible cash at 50% is assumed. The currency categories need checking with the team and the currency (FX) desks.
  • Money at stake: principal at risk is the full principal, not an expected loss.
  • Demo: one loan per borrower, fixed exchange rates, and everything resets on reload.
07 · SCOPE AND NEXT STEPS

What it won't do, and what comes next.

Non-goals
  • A real CRM or bank connector. The connector is simulated.
  • A full recoverability view. You enter a rough Strong, Partial or Weak score. No balance sheet or loan condition data.
  • A learned threshold. Fixed cut-offs: no past missed payments to learn from.
  • Automated actions. You keep every decision.
  • Forecasting money coming in, credit lines, borrowers with several loans, access control.
Next
  1. Test the forecast on past snapshots once their payment dates pass. This costs data, not users.
  2. Sit with two analysts and compare their order of work with the tool's list.
  3. A Salesforce connector in place of the simulated one.
  4. A four-week pilot to count warnings that end in a logged action.

Main learnings what the sample bank file and the demo showed
  • Sample bank file: 62 accounts, 22 currencies, $9,311,675 total. Only $795,714 (8.5%) sits in US dollars, euros or pounds (USD, EUR or GBP). This supports hypothesis 1. The deck said 82% was trapped; the file shows 91%.
  • Missing IDs: 17 of 62 rows have no account ID and hold $2,573,737 (28%). Without a way to build an ID, that cash drops out of the count.
  • Tests: demo/test.js runs 19 checks on 3 fixed borrowers plus 2 bank data events. 19 pass.
  • Demo event: a euro freeze at Agri A moves coverage from 1.04 to 0.38, Low to High, rank 3 to 1. This shows how the logic of hypotheses 1 and 2 works, not whether it's accurate.
  • Recall-first (warn early, accept more false alarms): Telecom C has coverage 1.02 but level Medium, because cash due sits above P40. The warning fires before coverage drops below 1.
  • Users: nothing observed. No analyst has used either the prototype or the demo.

All sub-problems the 8 found, and the 5 this build tackles
  1. Trapped cash. The cash total includes currencies the borrower can't convert or send, so coverage looks fine when it isn't.
  2. Future cash. Cash on the payment date is unknown today, and a single guess hides the bad case.
  3. Order of work. Each analyst handles borrowers in their own order, not by money at stake x recoverability.
  4. Trust. A warning nobody can check gets rebuilt in a spreadsheet or ignored.
  5. Follow-through. Actions and outcomes aren't recorded, so there's no history to set the threshold.
  6. Data plumbing. Bank balances and loan terms sit in separate systems, updated at different times.
  7. Recoverability. Whether a borrower can fix a shortfall, and what the lender gets back if not, needs financial statements, the lender's place in the repayment queue and the loan's conditions.
  8. Calibration. Setting the threshold needs past missed payments to learn from, and few exist.

This build tackles 1 to 5.

How it's built inputs, usable cash, range, level, rank
  1. Input. Simulated bank data (account, currency, balance in local currency, exchange rate, and how convertible the currency is) and a simulated CRM loan record (principal, interest rate, payments per year, share paid in cash, next payment date, recoverability).
  2. Usable cash. Local balance x exchange rate x a factor: 1 if freely convertible, 0.5 if partly convertible (assumed discount), 0 if blocked.
  3. Range on the payment date (T1). P50 = usable cash minus daily spending x days to T1. Spread = the usual daily swing in cash (standard deviation) x square root of days. P40 and P10 sit 0.25 and 1.28 spreads below P50.
  4. Cash due. Principal x interest rate / payments per year x cash share. A loan with all interest added to the loan (full PIK) owes no cash at T1.
  5. Coverage and band. Coverage = P50 / cash due. Band = P10 / due to P40 / due. This is the liquidity-at-risk method from the assignment deck: how low usable cash could fall in a bad case.
  6. Level. Cash due at or below P10: None. Up to P40: Low. Up to P50: Medium. Above P50: High. If bank data is more than 3 days old, the level is raised to at least Low.
  7. Rank and priority. Worst level first, then principal at risk, then coverage. High and Medium get P1 to P4, split by principal of $2M or more and recoverability score of 0.5 or more.
  8. Output. A ranked list, a trace of every number to its source field, and an action log.

Terms on this page plain definitions
  • Cash interest payment: interest the borrower must pay in money on a set date.
  • PIK (payment in kind): interest added to the loan instead of paid.
  • Usable vs trapped cash: cash the borrower can convert and send / cash stuck behind currency rules.
  • Coverage: expected usable cash on the payment date divided by the payment due. Below 1 means short.
  • P10, P40, P50: low, lower-middle and middle guesses of usable cash on the payment date.
  • Principal: the amount lent, before interest.
  • Recoverability: how likely the lender gets its money back.
  • Recall / precision: share of missed payments the tool warned about / share of warnings that were right.
