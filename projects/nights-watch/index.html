Night’s Watch

Flags private credit borrowers that may miss their next cash interest payment, then ranks the credit team's work by money at stake.

Private creditPortfolio monitoringProduct assignment, synthetic data

Problem

A lender can find a borrower's cash shortfall on the payment date, when the cheap fixes are gone, because headline cash includes money trapped in currencies the borrower can't send.

For

PlayerPrivate credit lender: direct loans with periodic cash interest, to borrowers holding cash in several currencies
UserCredit team analyst: which flagged borrower to work first today, and which action to take
BuyerHead of credit: the recall floor (how many false alarms to accept so no default arrives unflagged), and whether the team adopts the tool

User stories

  1. Rank. As a credit analyst, when a new bank feed lands, I want borrowers ranked by risk of missing their next cash coupon, worst first, so I work the biggest savable money first.
  2. Check. As a credit analyst, when a borrower is flagged, I want every number traced to the raw bank and loan record, so I can verify it before I call the borrower.
  3. Close the loop. As head of credit, I want each warning to end in a logged action, so I can see no flagged loan was left unattended and build the history that calibrates the threshold.

Try it

  1. Read the ranked list. Agri A sits at #3, level Low: $7.9M of cash, but only $481K in currencies it can send.
  2. Open its connector and replay FX freeze on EUR. Coverage drops from 1.04 to 0.38, the level goes Low to High, and Agri A moves to #1.
  3. Open Trace to see each number rebuilt from the simulated CRM and bank records, then Act to log a P1 action.

Synthetic borrowers, accounts, currency codes and FX rates. The CRM and bank-feed connector is simulated in the browser: no backend, no model call. A Salesforce (or other CRM) adapter and a bank-feed adapter are the next step.

Details

Value equation

value = (outcome x likelihood) / (time delay x effort & sacrifice)

CREDIT ANALYST

Outcome
"I open the queue and the borrower most likely to miss its coupon, with the most money at stake, is on top. I can show why in one click."
Likelihood
MEDIUM. Every number is traced. The forecast is a straight-line run-down, not tested against real missed payments. No analyst has used it.
Delay
LOW. The demo recomputes the whole portfolio on each feed event. Time from real feed to flag is unobserved: no live feed yet.
Effort
MEDIUM. No spreadsheet rebuild. Needs CRM fields kept current: next payment date, cash vs PIK, recoverability.
Sacrifice
MEDIUM. Gives up their own triage order for a house rule. One more screen next to the CRM.

HEAD OF CREDIT

Outcome
"No default catches us unaware, and the team works the most money at risk per day."
Likelihood
LOW-MEDIUM. Depends on bank-feed coverage, CRM completeness and a threshold calibrated on few labelled missed payments.
Delay
HIGH. Missed payments are rare, so recall takes quarters to measure.
Effort
HIGH. Bank and CRM adapters, data quality checks, access control and data protection review.
Sacrifice
MEDIUM. Borrower bank data sits in one more system. Recall-first means accepting more false alarms.

Verdict: likelihood is weakest for both.

  • Analyst fix: backtest the band. Each past snapshot labels itself once its payment date passes. If realised cash at T1 lands above P10 about 90% of the time, the band is calibrated. This costs data, not users.
  • Head of credit fix: shadow two analysts. Compare their triage order with the ranked queue on the same day. If the orders differ, ask which loans they would have worked first and why. This tests the ranking rule before any build.
Sub-problems
  1. Trapped cash. Headline cash includes currencies the borrower can't send, so coverage looks fine when it isn't.
  2. Future cash. Cash on the payment date is unknown today, and a single estimate hides the downside.
  3. Order of work. Each analyst works names in their own order, not by money at stake x recoverability.
  4. Trust. A flag nobody can check gets rebuilt in a spreadsheet or ignored.
  5. Follow-through. Actions and outcomes aren't recorded, so there's no history to calibrate the threshold.
  6. Data plumbing. Bank balances and loan terms sit in separate systems, refreshed at different rates.
  7. Recoverability. Whether a borrower can cure, and what the lender recovers if not, needs financials, seniority and covenants.
  8. Calibration. Setting the threshold needs labelled missed payments, and few exist.

This build tackles 1 to 5.

How it works
  1. Input. A simulated bank feed (account, currency, local balance, FX rate, convertibility class) and a simulated CRM loan record (principal, coupon, payments per year, cash share, next payment date, recoverability).
  2. Transferable cash. Local balance x FX x class factor: 1 for transferable, 0.5 for partial (assumed haircut), 0 for restricted.
  3. Band at T1. P50 = transferable cash minus burn per day x days to the next payment date (T1). Spread = daily net-flow standard deviation x square root of days. P40 and P10 sit 0.25 and 1.28 spreads below P50.
  4. Cash due. Principal x coupon / payments per year x cash share. A full PIK loan owes no cash at T1.
  5. Coverage and band. Coverage = P50 / cash due. Band = P10 / due to P40 / due. This is a liquidity-at-risk quantile, as in the assignment deck.
  6. Level. Cash due at or below P10: None. Up to P40: Low. Up to P50: Medium. Above P50: High. A feed older than 3 days is raised to at least Low.
  7. Rank and priority. Worst first by level, principal at risk, then coverage. High and Medium get P1 to P4: principal of $2M or more x recoverability score of 0.5 or more.
  8. Output. A ranked list, a trace of every number to its source field, and an action log.
Hypotheses, one per tackled sub-problem

1 · TRAPPED CASH

Before -> after
coverage on total cash -> coverage on transferable cash only
Hypothesis
If coverage counts only cash the borrower can send, then fewer borrowers look covered before a missed payment, because trapped cash can't pay a hard-currency coupon.
Metric
Share of missed payments where coverage was 1.0 or more one period before. Down. Baseline unobserved. Guardrail: false alarm rate (flags that end paid in full) stays under the cap the head of credit sets.
Test
On labelled history, compute coverage on total and on transferable cash. Count the misses each one flagged.

2 · FUTURE CASH

Before -> after
today's cash vs the coupon -> P10, P40, P50 band at the payment date
Hypothesis
If the flag depends on where cash due lands in the band, then more misses are flagged in time at the same false alarm rate, because the threshold sits in the downside tail, not the middle.
Metric
Recall: share of missed payments flagged at least N days before T1 (N set by the credit team). Up. Baseline unobserved.
Test
Backtest on held-out snapshots. Check calibration (realised cash above P10 about 90%, above P40 about 60%), then recall vs precision at each quantile.

3 · ORDER OF WORK

Before -> after
each analyst's own order -> worst first, then P1 to P4 by money x recoverability
Hypothesis
If the queue is sorted by level and principal at risk, then more principal at risk gets an action on the day of the flag, because the largest savable positions are worked first.
Metric
Principal at risk with an action logged within one day of the flag. Up. Baseline unobserved.
Test
Shadow two analysts' triage, then run the same day's queue in the tool. Compare order and principal covered in the first hour.

4 · TRUST

Before -> after
numbers rebuilt in a spreadsheet -> each number traced to its CRM or bank field
Hypothesis
If every number shows its formula and source field, then analysts act on flags sooner, because checking takes a click, not a rebuild.
Metric
Time from flag to first action. Down. Baseline unobserved. Also: share of flags overridden as wrong.
Test
Five flagged cases. Time to verify with raw files only vs with the trace.

5 · FOLLOW-THROUGH

Before -> after
actions in email and memory -> logged with level and coverage at the time
Hypothesis
If logging sits on the same screen as the flag, then more flags end in a recorded action, because the step costs one click, and each record becomes a calibration label.
Metric
Share of High and Medium flags with an action logged within the agreed time (deck: e.g. one day). Up. Baseline unobserved.
Test
Count over a four-week pilot.

Observed in the build
  • Sample bank export: 62 accounts, 22 currencies, $9,311,675 total. Only $795,714 (8.5%) sits in USD, EUR or GBP. Supports the premise of hypothesis 1. The deck said 82% trapped; the file shows 91%.
  • Missing IDs: 17 of 62 rows have no account ID and hold $2,573,737 (28%). Without a built key, that cash drops out.
  • Tests: demo/test.js runs 19 checks on 3 fixed borrowers plus 2 feed events. 19 pass.
  • Demo event: an EUR freeze at Agri A moves coverage from 1.04 to 0.38, Low to High, rank 3 to 1. This shows the mechanics of hypotheses 1 and 2, not accuracy.
  • Recall-first: Telecom C has coverage 1.02 but level Medium, because cash due sits above P40. The flag fires before coverage drops below 1.
  • Users: nothing observed. No analyst has used either the prototype or the demo.
Limits and trade-offs
  • Data: synthetic borrowers. The sample export is one point-in-time snapshot, so burn and runway can't be computed from it.
  • Users: untested. Every "before" is assumed.
  • Accuracy: unmeasured. Straight-line burn with normal noise is an assumption hypothesis 2 depends on.
  • Haircuts: partial convertibility at 50% is assumed. Classes need confirming with the team and FX desks.
  • Money at stake: principal at risk is the full principal, not an expected loss.
  • Demo: one loan per borrower, fixed FX rates, and state resets on reload.
