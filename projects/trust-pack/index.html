01 · VISION

Staff ask the family's documents anything, and the office can prove what each person saw.

Today an AI assistant shows the answer and hides how it got there. You can't tell what it read, or whether it read something the asker shouldn't see. With Trust Pack, every answer carries its evidence: who asked, what they could see, what was quoted, and a replay.

02 · THE PROBLEM

Five things go wrong today.

1 · The wrong person sees a family document

Many tools check access after the AI has written the answer. By then the model has read the text.

2 · A number appears with no source

A fluent sentence can sound right and come from nowhere. Nobody can check it.

3 · A planted note steers the assistant

A fund letter can carry text written for the AI. The assistant may follow it.

4 · ID and bank numbers come back out

KYC files hold passport, Emirates ID and IBAN numbers. Anything indexed can be quoted.

5 · Last month's answer can't be rebuilt

Logs keep the answer, not the passages and versions behind it.

03 · THE SOLUTION

Code decides who sees what. The answer is a checked quote. You can replay it.

What it is

A checking layer for an AI assistant that answers from a family office's documents.

What it does

Takes a question and who is asking. Returns a quoted answer from documents that person may see, or "not found", plus a record of every step.

What you get

Evidence you can show the principal: what each person could see, what was quoted, and the same answer again on replay.

06 · LIMITATIONS

What this doesn't prove yet.

  • Data: 23 short, made-up documents. Real ones have scans, tables and Arabic, and the search ignores Arabic text today.
  • Users: no family office has used it. There is no login: the user picker is a switch.
  • Accuracy: the offline engine answered 3 of 6 new answerable questions. The live AI path exists but is not measured yet.
  • Personal data: the finder misses 7 number formats, and names are never removed.
  • Audit: the log shows edits, but anyone with disk access can rewrite all of it.

How it's built 12 checks, from the document to the audit log
  1. Document contract: label, room, date, text and a fingerprint (SHA-256). 4 of 23 files rejected with a reason.
  2. Labels and personal data: public, confidential or restricted; ID and bank numbers replaced before indexing.
  3. Traceability: every passage keeps its document fingerprint, character range and versions.
  4. Where data goes: one config file; offline by default; one allowed outside address in live mode.
  5. Access check: the asker's rooms filter the passages before search ranks them.
  6. Search: keyword ranking (BM25) over the passages in scope.
  7. Guardrails: advice and override requests refused; planted notes skipped; quotes checked character for character.
  8. Test set: 8 questions to set the rules, 33 to test, 10 written after the rules froze.
  9. Release gate: 7 thresholds; make eval fails on any miss.
  10. Trace: one record per request with who, what, versions and timing.
  11. Audit and replay: a chained log; make replay rebuilds an answer and compares fingerprints.
  12. Runbook: find the trace, classify the failure, add it to the test set, fix, pass the gate.

The UAE data-law map which law applies depends on where the office is registered
  • Onshore: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (PDPL), in force since 2 January 2022. Its detailed rules appear unpublished as of late 2026. u.ae
  • DIFC: Data Protection Law No. 5 of 2020, with Regulation 10 on AI systems since 1 September 2023: notice at first use, purposes set by people, and a clear trigger for human review.
  • ADGM: Data Protection Regulations 2021, close to the GDPR, including rights around automated decisions. ADGM

A family office handles family members' data as a business, so household exemptions don't apply. Summarised for learning, not legal advice; checked 25 November 2026.

Terms on this page plain definitions
  • Room: a group of documents with one access list.
  • Passage: one paragraph of a document, the unit the search returns.
  • Fingerprint (SHA-256): a code computed from content; any change gives a different code.
  • Release gate: a set of minimum scores a change must pass before release.
  • Planted instruction: text inside a document written to steer an AI.
